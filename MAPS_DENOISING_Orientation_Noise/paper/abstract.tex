Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) orientation maps contain two kinds of
error: a small scatter of every measured orientation, and misindexed pixels
whose orientation is completely wrong. The filters commonly used to clean these
maps work grain by grain, so the grains must first be detected with a
threshold angle, and most of the filters have parameters that must be matched
to the noise level of the map. Isolated misindexed pixels are not repaired by
the filters themselves and have to be removed in a separate cleaning step. We
train a convolutional neural network that cleans an orientation map without
grain detection and without being told its noise level. For every pixel the
network decides how much each of its neighbours should count, after aligning
the neighbours across the 24 symmetry-equivalent forms of a cubic orientation,
and a separate output detects and rebuilds misindexed pixels. The network was
trained on about 39{,}000 synthetic DREAM.3D maps with randomly drawn noise
levels and tested on 500 other maps under 28 noise conditions, with maximum
scatter angles from $1^\circ$ to $15^\circ$ and 0 to 9\% misindexed pixels. In
every condition it removed 86--93\% of the mean per-pixel error. On test maps
with mixed noise levels the error fell from $5.69^\circ$ to $0.59^\circ$, and
94\% of the misindexed pixels ended within $5^\circ$ of their true orientation.
The standard deviation across three independent training runs was at most
$0.03^\circ$. In a first comparison on scatter-only maps, the network had a
lower mean error than the mean, median, Kuwahara, half-quadratic and
infimal-convolution filters of MTEX, and a lower but not significantly
different error than its spline filter. Because the method needs only the
orientation map, it can be applied to existing EBSD data, without the stored
diffraction patterns that pattern-based denoising requires.
